01 · The question

What is time?

We use clocks to measure time, but a clock measures change. Time is one of the fundamental concepts we use to describe when events happen and the order in which they happen.

Past

Events that have already happened relative to an observer.

Present

The moment we experience as “now”. What counts as a universal present is complicated by relativity.

Future

Events that have not yet occurred in an observer's experience.

“If nothing changed, there would be no physical event by which we could notice time passing.”
02 · Relativity

Einstein changed the picture.

In special and general relativity, time is not an identical universal clock shared by every observer. Space and time are combined into spacetime, and measurements of elapsed time depend on motion and gravity.

Time dilation

A moving clock can measure less elapsed time between events than a clock in another frame.

Gravity

Clocks at different gravitational potentials can accumulate different amounts of elapsed time.

Spacetime

Events are described using both spatial coordinates and a time coordinate.

This website presents simplified explanations for learning; relativity is more mathematically precise than these summaries.

03 · The arrow of time

Why does time seem to have a direction?

Many fundamental physical equations can work in both temporal directions, yet our everyday experience has a strong direction: we remember the past, not the future. Thermodynamics connects this everyday “arrow of time” with increasing entropy in an isolated system.

05 · Philosophy

Does the future already exist?

Philosophy of time contains competing views. Presentism treats the present as especially real, while eternalism treats past, present and future as parts of a larger spacetime picture. Relativity creates difficult questions for the idea of one universal “now”.

07 · Important distinction

Analogy ≠ proof.

The Time String is a way to visualize questions about time. A scientific theory needs mathematical definitions, predictions, evidence and testing. The goal of this project is to explore the questions first.
